# CrazyGames Submission — Super Important

The finished game will be submitted to CrazyGames.com. All development and final review must follow these quality guidelines alongside CrazyGames' mandatory requirements.

## Onboarding

- Send new players directly into a simple onboarding phase and get them to gameplay quickly.
- Teach controls and core functionality during gameplay.
- Keep onboarding focused on the core experience; do not explain every feature.
- Make onboarding skippable.
- Prioritize visual prompts and limit onboarding text.
- Show controls with keyboard overlays or mouse gestures while respecting restricted keys.
- Keep the UI clear.
- Label progression buttons clearly.
- Do not size buttons to encourage ads or unrelated behavior.
- Do not add confusing button delays.

## General principles

- Give players clear, reachable goals.
- Keep the game easy to learn and understand.
- Use correct, clear language, good translations, or universal visual prompts.
- Keep controls consistent and intuitive throughout the game.
- Respond quickly to player actions.
- Balance challenge, strategy, and story with good pacing.
- Keep the display layout comfortable and intuitive.
- Keep audio comfortable and appropriate.
- Design the interface for each supported device, including desktop and mobile.
- Make the game enjoyable for varied player segments.
- Keep stories and scenarios interesting where applicable.
- Avoid overly repetitive or boring tasks.
- Process information quickly to maintain smooth flow and continuity.

## Solo play and playing with friends

- If solo and multiplayer are both offered, make solo play as prominent as playing with friends.
- If solo play is unavailable, explain that clearly.

## Uniqueness and maintainability

- Structure the game so new levels, art, story elements, and other content can be added easily.
- Do not change major features such as the genre after submission.
- Maintain and update the game frequently.
- Use a unique name and iconography that cannot be confused with another game.
- Do not use common identifiers or third-party intellectual property unless the developer owns the relevant rights.
- Prefer a distinctive name such as “Super Chess” over a generic name such as “Chess.”

## Visual and audio quality

- Use high-quality, high-resolution graphics.
- Keep visual resolution consistent throughout the game.
- Remove compression artifacts and other graphical defects.
- Use high-quality audio with consistent volume levels.
- Ensure sounds are neither too loud nor too quiet.
- Use music that complements the visual experience.
- Keep the aesthetic internally consistent and coherent; do not switch unexpectedly between realistic and cartoon styles or between high- and low-resolution assets.
- Accurately represent the game's genre and experience in its name and CrazyGames imagery.
- Change the game's name or associated imagery only when necessary for a significant update or visual overhaul.

## Restricted keys and keyboard layouts

- Keep controls intuitive and easy to learn.
- Prefer bindings that adapt to the player's keyboard layout.
- Support layouts such as AZERTY, where typical WASD movement becomes ZQSD.
- Avoid browser-reserved key combinations and account for their behavior.
- Remember that Escape exits fullscreen.
- Remember that Ctrl/Cmd + W closes the browser tab; only attempt to suppress it while the player is in fullscreen.
